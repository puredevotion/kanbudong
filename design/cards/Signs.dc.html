Five signs, five objects

The card is not a plate that changes colour by topic. Each category is rendered as the kind of physical thing you actually meet it on, because recognising the object is half of reading the sign — you know a price label is a price label before you can read a character of it, and that knowledge is what makes the characters guessable.

01 · TRANSIT

Wayfinding plate · enamel, blue band, arrow

Highest-contrast of the five, because a station reads at distance and at speed. The exit letter is part of the sign, not decoration — it is what decides which street you come up on.

The only card with siblings: a dish means nothing without the rows around it and the section header above it. Reading a menu is reading a layout first. Serif face, because printed menus are set in Song, not Hei.

03 · SHOPFRONT

Fascia board · gold on red, heavy weight, inset rule

Deliberately the least legible of the five, and that is the lesson: a real fascia is loud, tightly tracked and often set in a display weight. If you can only read shopfronts when they are set cleanly, you cannot read shopfronts.

The one card where the character you must read (斤) is the smallest thing on it and the price shouts over it. That hierarchy is the trap the card is teaching, so it is reproduced rather than corrected.

05 · WARNING
小心地滑
CAUTION · SLIPPERY
Hazard board · yellow warns, red forbids

Colour carries meaning here in a way it does not on the other four, and the game should teach that directly: yellow asks you to take care, red tells you not to. Getting the colour convention is worth as much as getting the characters.

THE RULE

Whatever the treatment, one thing never changes: the characters are set the way the real object sets them. Never white-on-violet, never glowing, never rounded to match the app.

The dark chrome is the frame around the sign, not a skin applied to it. A learner who can only recognise 出口 in the app’s house style has learned the app, not the language.
